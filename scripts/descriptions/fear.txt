F.E.A.R. (First Encounter Assault Recon) is a critically acclaimed 2005 psychological horror first-person shooter developed by Monolith Productions and published by Sierra Entertainment. Renowned for its intense firefights, cutting-edge enemy artificial intelligence, and chilling J-horror-inspired narrative, the game remains a landmark title in the action-horror genre.

The game places players in the boots of the Point Man, a newly inducted operative in F.E.A.R., a secret military task force specialized in dealing with paranormal threats. The crisis begins when Paxton Fettel, a telepathically gifted commander, goes rogue and takes control of a battalion of clone soldiers, turning a major corporate conglomerate, Armacham Technology Corporation, into a battleground.

As the Point Man leads the tactical response to neutralize Fettel, he begins experiencing horrific, vivid hallucinations involving a creepy young girl in a red dress named Alma. As the investigation deepens, the line between reality and nightmare blurs, uncovering dark corporate secrets.

Key figures in the story include the Point Man, an unnamed protagonist with superhuman reaction times; Paxton Fettel, a cannibalistic telepath controlling the clone army; Alma Wade, a mysterious girl with immense psychic abilities locked away and experimented on by Armacham; and Armacham Technology Corporation itself, the ruthless defense contractor responsible for the breach.

As the Point Man pursues Fettel through derelict industrial complexes, corporate offices, and underground laboratories, he uncovers the tragic truth: Alma is his and Fettel's biological mother, and his incredible reflexes are the result of early bio-engineering experiments.

The game is remembered for several iconic mechanics. It featured revolutionary AI using Goal-Oriented Action Planning, where clone soldiers actively flank, cover each other, suppress player positions, and flush out targets with grenades. It also introduced a signature slow-motion gunplay system that emphasized cinematic environmental destruction, combined with an eerie J-horror atmosphere inspired by films like Ringu and Ju-On.
